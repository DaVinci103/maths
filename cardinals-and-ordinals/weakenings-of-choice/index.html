Weakenings of Choice

The axiom of choice, abbreviated AC, states that every surjection $f: A \twoheadrightarrow B$ has a section $s: B \to A$ ($s \circ f = \id_A$). In this chapter, we'll see a few weaker axioms, such as the axiom of countable and of dependent choice and CUT, and one stronger axiom: the axiom of global choice.

$\AC_\kappa$ and $\CC$

Given a cardinal $\kappa = |B|$, the proposition $\AC_\kappa$ or $\AC_B$ states:

For all $A$ and all surjections $f: A \twoheadrightarrow B$, there is a section $s: B \to A$.

Then, $\CC = \AC_{\aleph_0} = \AC_\omega$. The proposition $\CC$ is known as countable choice. For finite $n$, $\AC_n$ (finite choice) can be proven by induction. For $\kappa \le \lambda$, we have that $\AC_\lambda$ implies $\AC_\kappa$.

$\DC$

The axiom of dependent choice, or $\DC$, is the following proposition:

Let $X$ be a set and let $R \subset X \times X$ be a relation. If, for all $x \in X$, there is some $y \in X$ for which $x \mathrel{R} y$, then there is some sequence $f: \NN \to X$ for which, for all $i \in \NN$, $f(i) \mathrel{R} f(i+1)$.

For a given set $A$, $\DC(A)$ is $\DC$ for $X \subset A$. For example, $\DC(\RR)$ is the statement "for all $X \subset \RR$ and all $R \subset X \times X$, if $\forall x \in X \exists y \in X. x \mathrel{R} y$, then $\exists f: \NN \to X \forall i \in \NN. f(i) \mathrel{R} f(i+1)$".

Given a limit ordinal $\lambda$, we can strengthen $\DC$ to $\DC_\lambda$. A tree is a pair $(T,<)$ with a set $T$ and a binary relation $<$ on $T$ satisfying:

  • Transitivity. For $a,b,c \in T$, if $a < b$ and $b < c$, then $a < c$;
  • For $a \in T$, set ${\downarrow} a := \{b \mid b < a\}$. Then, $({\downarrow} a, <)$ is a well-order.

For $a \in T$, we define the rank $\rk a$ of $a$ as the order-type of ${\downarrow} a$. A branch of $T$ is a well-ordered subset $B \subset T$, maximal under the subset relation. The length $\lh B$ of a branch $B$ is its order-type. The height $\ht T$ of a tree $T$ is the supremum of lengths of its branches. A branch $B$ is cofinal iff $\lh B = \ht T$. An element $a \in T$ is a leaf, or is maximal, iff there is no $b \in T$ for which $a < b$. Then, $\DC_\lambda$ is the following statement:

Every height $\lambda$ has a leaf or a cofinal branch.

CUT

The countable union theorem, or CUT, is the following proposition:

For a set $A$ and a sequence of subsets $(A_n \mid n \in \NN)$, if each $A_n$ is countable and $A = \bigcup_{n \in \NN} A_n$, then $A$ is countable.

CUT is implied by CC and implies $\omega_1$ is regular. CUT is one of the weakest fragments of choice.

Partition Principle

The partition principle, or PP, is the following proposition:

For non-zero cardinals $\kappa$ and $\lambda$, if $\kappa \le^* \lambda$, then $\kappa \le \lambda$.

It is known that AC implies PP, though it is still open whether PP implies AC.

GC

Global choice, or GC, is the following proposition:

There is a (large) function \[\mathbf{c}: \prod_{X \in \Set} (X \to X)\] for which, for a set $X$ and for $a,b \in X$, we have $\mathbf{c}(X,a) = \mathbf{c}(X,b)$.

Global choice implies the axiom of choice: given a surjection $f: A \to B$, we can define the section $s: B \to A$ by setting $s(b)$ to be the unique element in the image of $\mathbf{c}(f^{-1}\{b\},-)$. It is known that GC is strictly stronger than AC, meaning that GC cannot be proven from AC.

Exercises

Assume CC. Show there are no Dedekind cardinals.

Show that DC implies CC.

Prove that $\DC$ and $\DC_\omega$ are equivalent.

Show that CC implies CUT and CUT implies $\omega_1$ is regular.
